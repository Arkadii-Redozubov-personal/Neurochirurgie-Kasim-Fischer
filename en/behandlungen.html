Surgical Procedures an der Halswirbelsäule: Dekompression, Fusion oder bewegungserhaltender Bandscheibenersatz (Arthroplastie) je nach individuellem Befund.

Percutaneous Tumour Radiofrequency Thermoablation

Percutaneous radiofrequency thermoablation is a minimally invasive procedure in which selected tumours or tumour-related lesions are targeted with thermal energy under imaging guidance. Whether this procedure is suitable depends on the type, size, and location of the lesion as well as the overall clinical situation, evaluated in an interdisciplinary setting.

Facet Joint Infiltrations, Blocks & Denervation

For pain originating from the facet joints of the spine, targeted diagnostic and therapeutic infiltrations or nerve blocks can be applied. When the pain origin is confirmed, radiofrequency denervation of the supplying nerve branches represents a further effective option. These procedures can be performed at various spinal levels depending on individual anatomy and indication.

Removal of Intraspinal Tumours

Tumours and space-occupying lesions within the spinal canal can compromise the spinal cord or nerve roots. Following thorough MRI diagnostics and neurosurgical evaluation, we assess the necessity and options for surgical treatment. The goal is safe decompression of neural structures with maximum preservation of neurological function.

Arthroplasty & Arthrodesis of the Sacroiliac Joint

In carefully selected patients with chronic sacroiliac (SI) joint complaints where conservative therapy has been exhausted, surgical options may be considered. Depending on clinical findings, stabilizing or fusion-based techniques are evaluated following targeted clinical, imaging, and interventional diagnostics.

Muscle Biopsy for Unclear Neuromuscular Conditions

In specific unclear neuromuscular disorders or neuropathies, a muscle biopsy can be a crucial part of differential diagnostics. A small tissue sample is harvested and analyzed specialized by our cooperation partners at Düsseldorf University Hospital (Uniklinikum Düsseldorf), coordinated with clinical, neurophysiological, and laboratory findings.

Neurosurgical Expert Assessment

When questions arise regarding treatment outcomes, iatrogenic injury, or suspected medical malpractice, an expert neurosurgical assessment can be conducted. Treatment records, imaging, timeline, and current findings are objectively evaluated to assess causality between previous medical interventions and reported health consequences.

Intervertebral discsoperation

Microsurgical and endoscopic procedures to relieve nerve root pressure

What is the goal of the operation?

The aim of the procedure is to remove herniated disc material using a surgical microscope or endoscope to relieve pressure on compressed nerve roots. Intervertebral disc material is removed via small accesses and with the help of special instruments. This technique aims to put as little strain on the surrounding muscles as possible.

Whether an operation is necessary depends on the course of the symptoms, neurological deficits and the results of the imaging. Many herniated discs are initially treated conservatively.

Typical procedure for a disc operation

1
Preparation

The diagnosis and indication for surgery are checked again. Before the procedure, the necessary medical and - depending on the anesthesia - anesthesiological information is given.

2
operation

The approach is chosen according to the affected spinal region. During decompression, the constricted nerve structure is relieved; Interfering intervertebral disc tissue can be specifically removed.

3
Immediately afterwards

After the procedure, circulation, pain and neurological findings are checked. Mobilization begins depending on the procedure and the individual course.

4
Discharge and follow-up care

Wound checks, stress build-up, if necessary physiotherapy and other checks are planned individually. Ability to work, exercise and driving will be discussed depending on the procedure.

Dringender Hinweis

Any new severe paralysis or disruption of bladder/intestinal function must be immediately clarified by a doctor.

Microsurgical decompression

Surgical relief for spinal canal stenosis and nerve root compression

When is decompression necessary?

If the spinal canal is narrowed (spinal canal stenosis), more space is created for the spinal cord and nerve roots by surgically removing constricting bone and ligament structures. If the symptoms remain significant despite appropriate conservative treatment or if relevant neurological problems exist, surgical relief can be considered.

Treatment depends on the extent and duration of the symptoms, neurological findings, imaging and personal goals. Whether additional stabilization is necessary is assessed individually.

Minimally invasive stabilization

In the event of instability (e.g. spondylolisthesis, spondylolisthesis), adjacent vertebral bodies can be connected to one another in a minimally invasive manner using special implant systems in order to prevent abnormal movements and permanently relieve pressure on nerve roots.

Percutaneous stabilization operations and dynamic stabilization procedures are established techniques that are used individually depending on the findings.

Dringender Hinweis

Any new severe paralysis or disruption of bladder/intestinal function must be immediately clarified by a doctor.

Kyphoplasty & Vertebral body stabilization

Minimally invasive treatment of vertebral body fractures and tumor-related changes

What is a Kyphoplasty?

In the event of a vertebral body fracture - for example due to osteoporosis, a fall or tumor infection - cement augmentation such as kyphoplasty can be considered in selected cases to stabilize the affected vertebral body. Medical bone cement is inserted into the fractured vertebral body to stabilize it and reduce fracture-related pain.

Whether the procedure is suitable depends in particular on the stability of the fracture, its cause, the symptoms, the neurological findings and the imaging. Not every vertebral fracture requires surgery.

Tumor-related changes & radiofrequency ablation

In the case of tumor-related changes to the spine, interdisciplinary treatment planning is particularly important. Depending on the situation, surgical, radiotherapy, medication and, in selected cases, ablative procedures can be combined.

Percutaneous radiofrequency thermal ablation should be seen as a possible option for certain findings (e.g. bone tumor metastases), not as a general standard therapy.

Surgical care peripheral nerves

Carpal tunnel syndrome, ulnar sulcus syndrome and other bottleneck syndromes

Carpal tunnel syndrome surgery

In carpal tunnel syndrome, the median nerve at the wrist is narrowed. The diagnosis results from complaints and clinical examination and can be supplemented by neurophysiological measurements.

If symptoms persist or there is significant nerve damage, surgical relief may make sense. The ligament over the carpal tunnel is cut to give the nerve more space. Open and endoscopic procedures are established; the appropriate method is selected individually.

Sulcus ulnaris syndrome & other bottleneck syndromes

In ulnar sulcus syndrome (cubital tunnel syndrome), the ulnar nerve is narrowed at the elbow. Tingling or numbness in the little finger and ring finger as well as pain on the inside of the elbow are typical. If the damage is advanced, muscle wasting and loss of strength in the hand may occur.

Nerves can also be compressed at the elbow and other anatomical narrow areas. By splitting the constricting band or tissue, the pressure on the affected nerve is relieved. Whether conservative or surgical treatment is indicated depends on the symptoms, clinical findings and, if necessary, neurophysiological results.

Neuromodulation

Special pain therapy & spinal cord stimulation (SCS)

What is Neuromodulation?

For selected chronic pain syndromes, neuromodulation, such as spinal cord stimulation (SCS), can be considered after careful consideration. The aim is to influence pain processing through targeted electrical stimulation of nerve pathways. Complete freedom from pain cannot be guaranteed.

In chronic, treatment-refractory pain syndromes, electrodes are placed in the epidural space. Light electrical impulses superimpose the pain signals on their way to the brain.

Sequence

1
Indication testing

Careful selection of suitable patients based on diagnosis, previous therapies and realistic therapy goals. Diagnosis, previous therapies, individual risks and realistic therapy goals are discussed in detail.

2
Test phase

Temporary implantation of electrodes to check effectiveness before inserting a permanent system.

3
Permanent implantation

If the test phase is positive, the permanent stimulation system is implanted and individually programmed.

Cervical spine surgery & Intervertebral disc prosthetics

Decompression, fusion and motion-preserving disc replacement

Diseases of the cervical spine

Changes in the cervical spine can affect nerve roots and, in certain cases, the spinal cord. Possible complaints range from neck and arm pain to numbness, reduced strength or fine motor problems.

Which operation is suitable?

Depending on the diagnosis, different procedures may be useful, including decompression, fusion or, in selected cases, a motion-preserving disc replacement.

A disc prosthesis is not suitable for every patient. Whether a prosthesis makes sense depends, among other things, on the affected segment, the extent of degenerative changes, stability, bone quality and other individual factors. The recommendation is only made after examination and image evaluation.

Arthroplasty & Arthrodesis

After removal of a degenerated intervertebral disc, under certain medical conditions, a prosthesis can be inserted (arthroplasty) with the aim of maintaining the mobility of the corresponding segment. Alternatively, neighboring vertebral bodies are permanently stabilized through a fusion (arthrodesis).
